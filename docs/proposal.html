Proposal

Make measured, publication-standard pottery drawings cheap and repeatable enough to do in the field, by automating everything that can be automated and being honest about what can't.

The problem

Pottery is the most abundant find on most archaeological sites, and a drawn profile (a cross-section showing the wall and its thickness) is the standard record of each vessel. Drawing one by hand is slow and varies between people. Specialist 3D tools such as GigaMesh produce excellent results but need desktop software, manual clicks per sherd, and a trained operator. That limits how many sherds are ever recorded to a measurable standard.

What the project does

  1. Capture cheaply. A consumer camera, a cardboard light tent, a turntable and printed scale markers. Hardware cost is about ₹3,000–4,300.
  2. Reconstruct. Photogrammetry (SfM/MVS) turns the photos into a 3D mesh. Calliper-measured distances between markers set the metric scale.
  3. Automate the drawing. Scripts clean the mesh, find the vessel's symmetry axis, cut the profile, measure wall thickness and publish a scaled SVG with the conventional line weight and scale bar.
  4. Gate the result. Every run ends in GO or REVIEW. Fragments the method can't pin down are sent to a person instead of being drawn badly.

Design principle: automate around the specialist tool

Most of the pipeline is routine and scriptable. One part is genuinely specialist: decorated-ware rollouts and faint-relief enhancement in GigaMesh. Rather than imitate that, the project keeps it as an optional manual station and automates everything around it. Plain, rotationally symmetric ware goes through the automatic lane end to end.

What is not yet shown

Real sherds. No accuracy figure on this site is from a photographed, broken sherd with a calliper-checked answer. That is the next and most important test. Until it is done, the numbers show the processing is correct, not that the whole chain reaches that accuracy.
  • Camera distortion on the Nikon AW100 is the largest known risk to metric accuracy.
  • The Stage 1 photo-to-mesh step is manual today.
  • Body sherds with no rim have no reliable automatic axis yet.

How accuracy will be proven

Take at least three independent photo sets of the same sherd, repositioning the sherd and markers between sets. Extract thickness at the same 5–10 points on every model and compare against digital-calliper readings (0.01 mm). Report mean difference and standard deviation, which separates repeatability within a session from reproducibility across sessions.

Roadmap

  1. Validate on real broken rim sherds against calliper measurements.
  2. Build the GPU lane (gpu/): COLMAP/OpenMVS runner, masking, automatic marker scaling.
  3. Improve small-arc and body-sherd axis estimation.
  4. Package as a notebook or container so it runs on any machine.
  5. Decide the production path (rented GPU, per-image licence, or a Metashape licence) once funded.
